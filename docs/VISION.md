# The Veilborn — Vision

> **A beautiful world that slowly teaches you not to trust what you see.**

## 1. The core idea

The Veilborn is a Minecraft Java Edition expansion that takes players beyond the familiar world into a strange dimension of ethereal floating islands, silver oceans, ancient monumental ruins, and shifting gravity.

At first, the dimension should feel wondrous: quiet horizons, impossible landscapes, and traces of a civilization that once understood this place. The longer players explore, the more that beauty should become unsettling. Landmarks may feel unfamiliar on a return visit, the rules of movement may not always behave as expected, and the ruins should raise more questions than they answer.

The experience is about **wonder turning into unease**, followed by the desire to discover what happened here.

## 2. The intended player experience

The player's journey should move through these broad emotional stages:

1. **Awe** — crossing the veil reveals a place unlike the Overworld or any familiar Minecraft dimension.
2. **Curiosity** — distant structures, unusual terrain, and environmental clues invite exploration.
3. **Unease** — the dimension's strange physical rules and abandoned monuments suggest that something is wrong.
4. **Discovery** — exploration, encounters, and progression gradually reveal pieces of the world's history.
5. **Confrontation** — powerful bosses challenge the player's understanding of the dimension, not just their equipment.
6. **Revelation** — the discoveries lead toward a larger truth. Its exact nature remains deliberately undecided in this overview.

These are design goals, not promises that every stage is implemented yet.

## 3. The atmosphere

The Veilborn should balance two qualities:

- **Ethereal beauty:** vast skies, floating landforms, silver water, unusual silhouettes, monumental architecture, and moments of stillness.
- **Growing unease:** impossible distances, unfamiliar environmental patterns, oppressive quiet, unexpected changes in gravity, and evidence of something missing.

Avoid making every location hostile or visually noisy. Quiet, beautiful spaces are important because they give the unsettling moments room to matter.

Horror should come primarily from atmosphere, uncertainty, scale, and implication—not constant jump scares.

## 4. Pillars of the experience

### A world worth exploring

The dimension should reward looking beyond the obvious route. Landmarks, terrain, ruins, and environmental details should encourage players to wonder what lies over the next horizon.

### Gravity as a world rule

Shifting gravity should be a defining feature, not merely a novelty. It must feel intentional and learnable enough for players to make decisions, while remaining strange enough to preserve the dimension's identity.

### A vanished civilization

The ruins should imply that their builders had a purpose, knowledge, and history. Players should piece together that history through places, objects, structures, and encounters rather than relying entirely on long exposition.

### Bosses with meaning

Boss encounters should be memorable and connected to the dimension's identity and progression. They should test observation and adaptation as well as combat ability.

### Mystery with restraint

Answers should arrive gradually. Early documentation and in-game clues must not explain every mystery immediately. Some major lore and late-game revelations are intentionally being kept undecided or private while the design develops.

## 5. Design boundaries

The Veilborn is intended to be a substantial Minecraft mod, not simply a server plugin or a collection of unrelated features.

- The dimension should have a coherent visual and gameplay identity.
- New mechanics should reinforce the world's atmosphere and exploration.
- Progression should give players reasons to travel, investigate, prepare, and return to earlier discoveries.
- Difficulty should feel fair: unusual rules need readable clues and opportunities to learn.
- Features should be developed in stages; planned ideas must not be described as already implemented.
- The first release does not need to contain the entire ultimate vision.

## 6. Technical commitment

- **Game:** Minecraft Java Edition 26.3 is the primary target.
- **Fallback:** Minecraft Java Edition 26.2 may be considered only if reasonable paths to 26.3 have been exhausted. Do not downgrade below 26.2.
- **Mod loader:** Fabric is the current choice, not a permanent requirement. Another loader may be considered if it better supports the target game version and project needs.
- **Mod ID:** `veilborn` (planned).
- **Release versioning:** Semantic Versioning (SemVer), with prereleases such as `0.1.0-alpha.1`.

When a technical obstacle appears, first investigate compatible loader versions, dependencies, APIs, and code-level solutions before considering the fallback game version.

## 7. What success looks like

A successful Veilborn experience leaves players remembering more than its loot or boss fights. They should remember the first impossible horizon, the strange rules of the dimension, the scale of the ruins, the feeling that the world has a history they do not yet understand, and the desire to return to uncover more.

The guiding question for new features is:

**Does this make The Veilborn more wondrous, more mysterious, more coherent, or more rewarding to explore?**

If not, it may not belong.
