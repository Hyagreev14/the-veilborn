# The Open Expanse — Generation

> **Status:** Generation direction agreed at a concept level. Numeric settings, biome placement, and implementation remain undecided.

## Terrain identity

Generate a landscape of **broad floating landmasses separated by open-sky gaps**. The biome should feel spacious rather than like a dense maze of small platforms.

Possible terrain forms:
- Wide, gently rolling island tops with enough continuous ground for unhurried exploration.
- Broken edges, exposed cliffs, and occasional narrow projections that create route choices.
- A small number of detached rocks or minor islands to add depth without filling every gap.
- Distinctive natural silhouettes visible from a distance, such as tall rock spires or unusual island profiles.

These are terrain directions, not a requirement that every island contain every feature. The exact surface material and vegetation palette are open.

## Scale and variation

Island size, elevation, and spacing should vary. Avoid both extremes:
- A single flat plane with little reason to explore.
- A field of tiny, disconnected platforms that turns every movement into a precision challenge.

The player should usually be able to identify a plausible destination before committing to a crossing, even if the safest route takes longer.

## Placement and rarity

The biome's overall frequency, vertical distribution, and average size are undecided. It is intended to provide a recognizable open landscape within the dimension, but it is **not confirmed as the entry biome or the first biome in progression**.

Biome placement should support a coherent world rather than generate abrupt, arbitrary borders. The dimension's hybrid open-world plan allows distant or concealed regions to be more dangerous, but distance and danger should not be treated as the same thing.

## Transitions

Potential transitions include:
- Gradually increasing rock fragmentation toward The Shattered Heights.
- Lower or more distant terrain leading toward regions associated with the Silver Sea, if the world layout supports it.
- Increasingly unusual silhouettes or local effects near other specialized regions.

These are examples only. Exact neighbor relationships are not finalized, and transitions should be designed around the eventual world-generation layout.

## Features and points of interest

Natural landmarks should be the primary navigational language. Small discoveries or optional points of interest may be added if they strengthen exploration.

Large ancient ruins belong primarily to the dimension's broader structure design and to the identity of The Fallen Monuments; do not fill the Open Expanse with major ruins merely to make it feel populated.

No unique structures, flora, fauna, ore placements, or block sets are finalized here.

## Technical notes

- Avoid excessive counts of tiny floating pieces if they create unnecessary generation or rendering cost.
- Preserve meaningful negative space between islands.
- Ensure generation does not create large areas with no reasonable route onward.
- Decide exact spacing, island dimensions, height ranges, and feature frequency only after the broader dimension-generation strategy is clearer.
