# The Open Expanse — General Design

> **Status:** Biome name and broad identity agreed. Floating islands and flowing silver water are core visual pillars; exact generation, assets, and implementation remain provisional.

## Core identity

A vast sky filled with **floating islands at different heights and distances**, with streams and waterfalls of mysterious silver water spilling from their edges into the open air. The islands—not a continuous ground plane—are the landscape.

The Open Expanse should deliver the feeling of an entire world suspended in the sky: beautiful, quiet, and inviting, with distant landmasses and falling rivers making the scale feel almost impossible.

## Purpose in the dimension

The Open Expanse is the dimension's **open-navigation reference biome**. Its floating landmasses, long sightlines, and visible watercourses let players read the landscape, choose routes, spot distant points of interest, and gradually learn the scale of The Veilborn.

It is not automatically the dimension's entrance biome; the secret entry method and exact progression order remain intentionally undefined.

## Signature features

- **Floating islands everywhere:** The main terrain consists of separate landmasses suspended in the sky, not ordinary continuous ground with a few floating rocks.
- **Different island scales:** Huge islands provide room to explore; medium islands add route options; smaller fragments add depth and may form stepping-stone paths.
- **Rocky undersides:** Islands have visible irregular undersides, cliffs, overhangs, and exposed stone, so they read as three-dimensional landmasses rather than flat platforms.
- **Silver streams and waterfalls:** The dimension's distinctive silver water flows across island surfaces, over ledges, and down into open air. Some falls may disappear into clouds or distant haze; whether particular flows connect to the Silver Sea remains a world-generation decision.
- **Long sightlines:** Distant islands, falling ribbons of water, and natural silhouettes make the world feel immense.
- **Open-sky gaps:** The spaces between islands are fundamental to exploration and encourage deliberate route planning.
- **A mostly legible baseline:** Gravity and traversal should generally be predictable here, making unusual regions elsewhere feel meaningfully different.

These are core design directions, not a locked feature checklist for every individual island.

## Intended player experience

1. **Look outward:** See islands at multiple heights, with silver water spilling from some of them.
2. **Read the landscape:** Use island shapes, cliffs, and waterfalls as visual landmarks.
3. **Choose a path:** Decide whether to follow a broad island, cross a gap, or investigate a watercourse or distant silhouette.
4. **Explore at the player's pace:** Find small discoveries and optional points of interest without constant pressure.
5. **Notice the strange:** The water's unusual appearance and the sight of rivers falling into open sky hint that this world follows unfamiliar rules.

The biome should reward curiosity and orientation, not require a single correct route.

## Distinction from other biomes

- The Open Expanse emphasizes **floating islands, open views, and silver waterfalls**.
- The Silver Sea emphasizes the dimension's mysterious silver ocean and its broader water environment.
- The Shattered Heights emphasizes fractured high terrain and more complex vertical routes.
- The Gravity Fracture emphasizes conspicuous disruption of gravity.
- The Fallen Monuments emphasizes large-scale ancient architecture.

These distinctions are role guidelines, not rigid borders. A distant ruin or unusual gravity event could appear at a boundary, but should not erase the biome's primary identity.

## Resources and progression

No unique block, ore placement, creature, structure, or equipment requirement is finalized for this biome. Veilcryst and Aetherium generation must be decided as part of the wider progression design, not assumed from this page.

The biome should remain explorable without requiring a specific Veilborn equipment set.

## Open decisions

- Final island surface palette, vegetation, and natural landmark language.
- How silver water behaves, including whether it uses vanilla water, a custom fluid, or a visual/environmental implementation.
- Whether waterfalls terminate in clouds, continue through lower island layers, reach the Silver Sea, or use a combination of these.
- How common watercourses are and how much terrain they carve or cover.
- How frequent and wide the open gaps should be.
- Its distribution, size, and transitions to neighboring biomes.
