# The Veilborn — Dimension Design

> **Status:** Initial design. Choices establish a direction, not permanent commitments. Details may change as the project develops.

The Veilborn is a custom Minecraft Java Edition dimension that should feel unmistakably different from the Overworld, Nether, and End: vast, quiet, ethereal, and filled with evidence of a civilization whose history is not yet understood.

## Core identity

The dimension is built around five connected ideas:

- A sky that changes character across the world.
- Islands and ruins arranged on a range of scales and heights.
- Silver oceans that may take several forms.
- Gravity that can vary by region, shift in cycles, or be influenced by ancient structures.
- An entry method that remains deliberately undisclosed while the design develops.

The world should invite exploration through beauty first, then gradually introduce uncertainty. Not every location should be frightening; calm, open spaces make unusual events more meaningful.

## Sky and atmosphere

The sky should not look identical everywhere. Possible regional treatments include pale clouds and soft illumination, unusually deep star-filled vistas, or subtle distortions that suggest space does not behave normally. These are possible variations, not requirements to show every effect at once.

## Island geography

Island scale and arrangement should vary.

- **Small fragments:** isolated rocks and broken platforms can create traversal routes and discoveries.
- **Major floating islands:** larger landmasses provide room for exploration, distinct environments, resources, and substantial ruins.
- **Vertical layers:** some regions may form higher reaches, intermediate island chains, and deeper or more concealed spaces.

Not every region needs all three scales. Variation in island density, elevation, and distance should make exploration diverse and purposeful.

## Silver oceans

Silver water is a signature feature, but it need not behave identically everywhere. The design may use different expressions of water and suspended liquid, provided the world remains coherent. Whether these are vanilla water, a custom fluid, or visual/environmental effects remains an open technical and design question.

## Gravity

Gravity is a central world rule and exploration tool. It may vary by region, occur in localized anomalies, shift in cycles, or respond to ancient structures. Any effect should be understandable enough for players to learn and use, not feel like arbitrary punishment.

## Ancient civilization

Ruins should communicate history through architecture, arrangement, mechanisms, and environmental traces. The dimension's mysteries should be revealed gradually. This document intentionally does not define the secret entry method or unrevealed boss lore.

## Exploration and progression

Exploration should connect discovery, resource gathering, learning the dimension's rules, and restoring selected ancient systems. Progression may produce lasting visible changes as well as temporary effects, while avoiding penalties for leaving or logging out at an inconvenient time.

## Design principles

- Beauty and unease should coexist.
- Landmarks should be recognizable from a distance where useful.
- Different regions should have meaningful terrain and gameplay identities.
- Gravity changes should be readable and purposeful.
- Multiplayer world state should remain consistent for everyone.
- Performance and practical implementation should shape ambitious ideas.

## Open questions

- Exact dimension entry and world-generation implementation (entry method remains secret)
- Silver ocean behavior and fluid implementation
- Final biome list and distribution
- Detailed gravity rules and transitions
- Structure generation and restoration stages
